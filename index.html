Scientific Methodology

The Mathematics Behind ZScope

ZScope implements well-established electrochemical impedance theory. The key equations governing the modeling and fitting engine are shown below.

Impedance Response

$$Z(\omega) = Z'(\omega) - j Z''(\omega)$$

The complex impedance $Z$ at angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$ is decomposed into its real (resistive) and imaginary (reactive) components, giving the Nyquist representation when plotted as $-Z''$ vs $Z'$.

Constant Phase Element (CPE)

$$Z_\text{CPE}(\omega) = \frac{1}{Q(j\omega)^n}$$

The CPE generalizes the ideal capacitor ($n=1$) and resistor ($n=0$). For $n \approx 0.8$–$0.95$, it models distributed surface capacitance due to electrode roughness or heterogeneous reaction kinetics. ZScope fits both $Q$ and $n$ simultaneously.

Warburg Diffusion Impedance

$$Z_W(\omega) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\omega}}\left(1 - j\right)$$

Semi-infinite Warburg diffusion introduces a 45° phase angle at low frequencies. ZScope also implements the finite-length Warburg (FLW) and finite-space Warburg (FSW) variants for bounded diffusion layers in batteries and fuel cells.

Weighted Complex Least-Squares

$$\chi^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left[ \frac{\left(Z'_k - \hat{Z}'_k\right)^2 + \left(Z''_k - \hat{Z''}_k\right)^2}{|Z_k|^2} \right]$$

ZScope minimizes the modulus-weighted sum of squared residuals, which normalizes contributions across the frequency range and is recommended by the EIS community for unbiased parameter extraction.

Bayesian Posterior (MCMC)

$$p(\boldsymbol{\theta} \mid \mathbf{Z}) \propto p(\mathbf{Z} \mid \boldsymbol{\theta})\, p(\boldsymbol{\theta})$$

MCMC sampling explores the full posterior distribution of circuit parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ given the measured impedance data $\mathbf{Z}$. This yields credible intervals, correlation structure, and uncertainty propagation that least-squares alone cannot provide.

Kramers–Kronig Relations

$$Z'(\omega) = R_\infty + \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{\xi Z''(\xi)}{\xi^2 - \omega^2}\,d\xi$$

Causality and linearity require the real and imaginary parts of $Z$ to satisfy the Kramers–Kronig relations. The lin-KK method fits a superposition of RC elements to test compliance without assuming an equivalent circuit model.

Workflow

From Raw Data to Publication

A four-step workflow designed for reproducible electrochemical analysis.

01

Import Spectrum

Load data from Gamry, BioLogic, Autolab, Solartron, Ivium, CSV, or Excel — automatic format detection with frequency range preview.

02

Validate with KK

Run lin-KK compliance test before fitting to confirm the spectrum is causal, linear, and stationary. Residual maps highlight problematic frequency ranges.

03

Build & Fit Model

Construct the equivalent circuit visually. Run TRF fitting with multi-start initialization, then optionally quantify uncertainty with Bayesian MCMC.

04

Export & Publish

Generate a reproducible fit report with parameter table, confidence intervals, residual diagnostics, and publication-ready figures.

Research Applications

Built for Real Electrochemistry

ZScope has been designed to handle the equivalent circuit models and data characteristics encountered across electrochemical research disciplines.

Battery Research

Characterize charge-transfer resistance, solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation, Warburg diffusion, and degradation mechanisms in Li-ion, Na-ion, and solid-state batteries.

Typical circuit: R(CR)(CW) · R(C(RW))

Perovskite Solar Cells

Investigate recombination dynamics, ionic transport, capacitive behavior at transport layers, and charge-transfer kinetics at perovskite–contact interfaces.

Typical circuit: R(CR)(CR) · R(C(RC))

Corrosion Science

Extract polarization resistance $R_p$, coating capacitance, pore resistance, and diffusion-controlled kinetics for corrosion inhibition and protective coating studies.

Typical circuit: R(Q(R(QR))) · Randles

Fuel Cells & Electrolyzers

Separate ohmic losses, charge-transfer resistance, and mass transport limitations. Resolve anode and cathode contributions in PEM and alkaline systems.

Typical circuit: R(QR)(QR)W

Supercapacitors & EDLCs

Characterize the electric double layer, porous electrode transmission line behavior, and frequency-dependent capacitance in carbon-based and pseudocapacitive materials.

Typical circuit: R(QR) · Transmission line

Biosensors & Bioelectrochemistry

Analyze electron transfer at modified electrode surfaces, detect binding events through impedance changes, and model protein–electrode interfaces for biosensor development.

Typical circuit: R(C(RW)) · Modified Randles
